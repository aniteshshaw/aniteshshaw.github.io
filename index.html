AI agents for quality engineering 60–70% less analysis & design effort

QIS: putting senior QA judgment into AI agents

In a 30+ engineer organization, the best story reviews and test designs came from a handful of senior people, and they were stretched thin. I built QIS on GitHub Copilot and MCP: a set of agents and reusable skills for story readiness, test case design, Playwright automation, PR review, evidence linking, defect triage, incident RCA and accessibility checks. Every agent looks things up in the real code and tracker before it answers, and a person approves what it produces. Dev and QE squads adopted it, analysis and design effort dropped 60–70%, and it won a Technology Innovation Award. The playbooks later became my own product, QIS-Skills.

Release readiness 70–75% fewer false defect reports

A readiness signal people could trust

Release calls kept getting muddied by "defects" that turned out to be a broken dependency or a sick environment. I built a service dependency and readiness dashboard that shows which upstream services are healthy before anyone files a bug. The first version shipped in under a week. False defect reports fell 70–75%, each incident takes 4–6 fewer hours to investigate, and together with shift-left changes to how we test, the program has held around 98% customer satisfaction with no escalations.

Small tools, daily payoff Hackathon win · setup 25 min → 3

An AI triage agent and a one-command setup

Defect triage and test-environment setup were eating hours every week. I built an AI triage agent that reads a new issue, summarizes it and suggests a likely root cause. It won an internal AI hackathon. I also wrote a PowerShell installer that took environment setup from about 25 minutes to two or three.

About

A bit more about me

I started in 2007 writing PL/SQL for a telecom company and moved into testing two years later. I've stayed in quality ever since: six years with a global bank, four of them onsite in the US, then QA leadership on large programs for a global professional services firm, and now a QE organization of more than thirty people at a North American logistics company.

The last couple of years changed the job. AI can write a test suite in a minute, and someone still has to decide whether it's any good. That someone is usually QA. So I've spent that time building the agents, setting the guardrails, measuring what changed, and teaching the patterns to the people around me. I've come to think of the role less as a gatekeeper and more as a city planner. You lay out the roads so good work flows and bad work gets caught early.

Outside work I build my own products, A2Quest, Bhaktiverse and QIS-Skills, and I'm finishing a draft of AI-Native Quality Engineering. I'm also a DHS Trusted Tester, so accessibility is never far from how I think about quality.
